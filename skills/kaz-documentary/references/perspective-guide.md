# "What the evidence shows": the closing perspective segment

This is the channel's own reading of the case, in the user's voice. It is the clearest sign that a person, not a template, made the video. Claude drafts it; the user edits it, approves it and records it (their real voice, or their ElevenLabs clone if that is how they narrate). It is the user's perspective, so never record or publish it without their approval, and invite them to change anything they do not agree with.

## Drafting rules

- 150-220 words (about 70-90 seconds), first person ("I", "to me"), plain and calm. It sits after the open questions and before the subscribe line.
- Build it only from facts already established and sourced in the script. No new claims.
- Sort the evidence into three short parts:
  1. **What is established**: two or three facts that no serious source disputes.
  2. **What is contested**: the strongest allegation and the strongest reason to doubt it, with who says what.
  3. **What would settle it**: the specific record, test or testimony that is still missing.
- Give a judgment about the evidence, not a verdict about people: "the accident explanation no longer fits the evidence on its own" is fine; "X killed him" is not, unless a court has convicted X. Never present an allegation as fact; never accuse a living person of a crime.
- End on a line the user can say naturally, then the subscribe line follows.
- Offer one alternative sentence for the judgment line (firmer and softer), so the user can choose how strongly to put it.

## Delivery

- Send the draft as text in the chat for the user to edit; once approved, add it to the script where `[PERSPECTIVE]` stands.
- Real voice (`host_bookends` = `both` or `outro_only`): the user records it as `outro.wav` (a quiet room, the same mic as any intro). Otherwise it is part of the last chapter's narration.
- On the board, show it plainly: the camera settles on the board's key items with the tags off, a small mono caption "WHAT THE EVIDENCE SHOWS · [USER'S NAME OR CHANNEL]", and no new marks, so it reads as commentary, not evidence.
